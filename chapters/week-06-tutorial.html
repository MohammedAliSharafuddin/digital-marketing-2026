Your tutorial session runs for two hours. You will conduct a structured SEO audit of your campaign’s primary landing page or content hub, identifying keyword cluster opportunities, on-page and local signal gaps, and internal-linking requirements aligned to your Week 2 canvas. Your week has nine hours of structured learning time in total: one hour of lecture, two hours of tutorial shown above, and six hours of self-directed study covered in the independent study chapter.

AI-Assisted SEO Protocol

AI can support the Week 6 SEO task in two ways. First, AI can generate lists of related keywords and related questions for a target topic, which you then validate against a public keyword tool. Second, AI can audit on-page content against a target keyword, identifying where the keyword and related terms are present and where the content has gaps relative to similar pages.

Restrictions apply: search volume data must always come from a public tool with a date cited, since AI training data lags behind current search figures and any volume number it produces is a guess dressed as a fact. Treat AI-generated keyword suggestions as a starting point for research, one you still verify against a real tool.

Tutorial Task

In your two-hour tutorial, you will conduct a first-draft SEO mini-audit. Choose a real or hypothetical URL for your campaign’s primary content page. If no page exists, describe the page you would create.

Work through these steps in sequence.

  1. From your Week 2 canvas, identify the hierarchy stage and target segment. Use Table 2 from the lecture to determine which intent type your primary content should target.
  2. Use Google, Google’s “People Also Ask” panel, AlsoAsked, and AnswerThePublic to gather seven or more queries related to your campaign topic. Record the search volume or relative interest and the intent type for each.
  3. Group the captured queries by shared intent into clusters, following the method in the lecture’s Keyword Clustering section. Select one primary cluster and two support clusters for your target page.
  4. Draft an H1 and H2/H3 structure for the target page, built around the primary cluster term with the two support terms worked into genuine subtopics, plus a title tag and meta description snippet.
  5. Map three internal links from the target page to other pages on the same domain. If your campaign serves a geographically bounded audience, check NAP consistency across the website, Google Business Profile, and any directory listing, and note any local page actions needed.
  6. As a secondary check, search the domain in a public tool such as Ubersuggest or Moz Link Explorer and record the number of referring domains and the estimated domain authority score.

Activities 1 to 4 each carry their own table, and every one shows how the Reef Edge Guesthouse example filled in the same row, directly alongside the blank cell where you fill in yours. Read Reef Edge’s entry, then write the equivalent for your own campaign in the same amount of detail. By Activity 5 those four sets of tables are your finished mini-audit, with no separate summary table to copy everything into again.

Mark any cell [ASSUMPTION] where the entry rests on belief rather than tool data, then state a testing method.

In-Class Activities

Activity 1: Orientation (10 minutes)

Format: Whole class | Output: Three-question concept check result and audit scope note

Complete the three-question Moodle concept check individually and without notes.

NoteMoodle Concept Check: Week 6

Open the Week 6 Concept Check quiz in Moodle under the Week 6 activities panel.

Question 1. Information foraging theory predicts that searchers will:

  1. Browse randomly until they encounter relevant content
  2. Optimise the cost of finding information against its expected value, selecting queries and sources strategically
  3. Always prefer the first result on the page regardless of relevance
  4. Prefer image-based content over text-based content in search results

Question 2. A campaign in a small market wants to rank for the query “guesthouse Maldives.” According to the long tail principle, what is the primary problem with targeting this keyword?

  1. The keyword has too few monthly searches to generate useful traffic
  2. The keyword’s intent is too specific for an Awareness-stage campaign
  3. The keyword faces competition from established travel platforms and price-comparison sites with greater authority than the campaign can realistically build
  4. The keyword is too long to fit in a title tag

Question 3. A search query with transactional intent is most likely to include which of the following terms?

  1. “What is” or “how does”
  2. “Best” or “compare”
  3. “Apply”, “enrol”, or “buy”
  4. A brand name or website name

After the concept check, your tutor walks through one exemplar SEO mini-audit so you can see a finished decision record before starting your own. Confirm the upload filename rule now, before you build anything: W06_[YourName]_SEOAudit.pdf.

Activity 3: Cluster & Brief (40 minutes)

Format: Individual | Output: A keyword cluster, and a draft H1/H2/H3 structure with a title tag and snippet, entered in your decision record

Using Figure 4 and Table 2 from the lecture, group your Activity 2 terms by shared intent, following the clustering method in the lecture. Select one primary cluster term and two support cluster terms for your target page.

Then draft the page’s H1 (built around the primary term) and an H2/H3 structure that works the two support terms into genuine subtopics rather than repeating the primary term. Draft a title tag (50-60 characters, primary term included, differentiated from competitors in the same results page) and a meta description snippet.

Count the characters of your title tag and meta description, and write the counts down. A title that runs long truncates in the results page, and the differentiator is usually what gets cut.

TipThe cluster test

Before finalising your H2/H3 structure, ask: does each subheading answer a genuinely different question, or does it just restate the primary term in different words? A structure that repeats the same claim three times under three headings has padded one keyword three times, over clustering it.

Part B: Your own page (10 minutes, individual)

Map three internal links from your target page to other pages on the same domain, and record the link count and relevance in your decision record. Use the anchor text standard you have just applied to someone else’s site: every anchor should name its destination.

If your campaign serves a geographically bounded audience (a Malé-based programme, a guesthouse, a local service), check NAP consistency: does the business name, address, and phone number match exactly across the website, the Google Business Profile listing, and any directory listing? Note any local page action needed (claiming an unclaimed profile, correcting a mismatched address) in your decision record. If your campaign has no geographic dimension, mark this row [NOT APPLICABLE] and state why in one sentence.

As a secondary check, search your target domain in a free tool (Ubersuggest, Moz Free Link Explorer, or Ahrefs Free Backlink Checker) and record the number of referring domains and the estimated domain authority score. Run your page through PageSpeed Insights as well, and record the mobile score.

Activity 5: Share & Upload (10 minutes)

Format: Room presentations, then individual | Output: 90-second presentation, completed self-check, uploaded PDF

Room presentations (7 minutes)

Each room presents for 90 seconds, no slides. The Recorder speaks. Cover three things in this order:

  1. **“We would fix ___ first, because ___.”**
  2. “We would fix ___ second.”
  3. **“We would leave ___ alone, because ___.”**

Listen for where rooms disagree. Two rooms reading the same nine findings and reaching different orders is the point of the exercise, since it means the ordering came from judgement rather than from the report.

TipWhat the class comparison usually reveals

Rooms that pick the favicon are reading colour: it is a hard FAIL, so it looks urgent. Rooms that pick the title tag are reading consequence: it is only a warning, yet it decides whether the result gets clicked at all. An audit tool ranks by rule breach. You rank by commercial impact, and the two orders rarely match.

Self-check and upload (3 minutes)

Combine your completed Activity 1, 2, 3, and 4 tables, in order, into a single PDF: that combined document is your SEO mini-audit. Then complete the five-point self-check below.

Check Pass or Needs Work Your comment
1. Canvas traceability. The primary cluster term is matched to the hierarchy stage and intent type implied by the Week 2 canvas.
2. Keyword evidence. Search volume or relative interest for all three cluster terms is documented from a named public tool with the query and date. No data is invented or sourced from AI output.
3. Cluster structure. The H2/H3 structure works the two support terms into genuine subtopics.
4. Local and authority signals. The local signals check is completed or marked [NOT APPLICABLE] with a reason; the domain authority score and referring domain count are from a named tool with an access date.
5. Assumption labelling. Every audit element without documented data is labelled [ASSUMPTION] with a proposed testing method.

Upload format: PDF named W06_[YourName]_SEOAudit.pdf, uploaded to the Week 6 Tutorial Submission folder in Moodle before leaving the session.
